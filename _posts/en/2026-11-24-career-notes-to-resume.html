You remember the project you worked on. You know which tools you used, and you can explain what you did during your internship.

Then you open a blank resume document, and suddenly none of it sounds right.

Should the project come before your part-time job? Does helping a team prepare reports deserve a bullet point? How do you describe useful work when there’s no impressive number attached to it?

Those questions can keep a first resume unfinished for weeks.

With NoteGPT’s AI Resume Builder, you can provide your background and a target role to generate a draft for review. Getting that first version onto the page gives you something to work with: experience you can reorder, descriptions you can sharpen, and gaps you can fill.

The process starts with the information you bring.

Start with the facts you already have

Gather a few notes about your education, work experience, projects, tools, and responsibilities. Include results you can verify, but don’t worry about making every sentence sound like it belongs on a resume.

For each experience, ask yourself:

  • What did I do?
  • What tools did I use, or who did I work with?
  • What did the work produce or help accomplish?

“Updated an Excel spreadsheet every Friday using sales figures from three stores” gives you more material to work with than “strong analytical skills.” It names a task, a tool, and the scope of the work.

If your background includes a university assignment, an internship, and a part-time job, record all three before deciding what belongs in the final document. An experience that seems minor at first may contain a useful example of research, coordination, or customer communication.

Keep these notes as a separate master record. You’ll want to return to them when applying for another role.

Give NoteGPT a specific role and useful context

The position you’re applying for should guide what you emphasize.

For a marketing assistant role, campaign research and writing might deserve more space. For an operations role, the same applicant might lead with reporting and coordination.

Before generating a draft, put your target role and relevant background together in the input. If you have a job description, include it alongside your own information so the requirements have context.

Here’s a hypothetical example you can adapt:

Create a first resume draft for an entry-level marketing assistant role.

My background includes a business degree, a three-month internship helping prepare weekly campaign reports in Excel, a university project researching customer preferences, and a part-time retail job answering customer questions.

Emphasize relevant research, reporting, and communication experience. Use concise language. Do not invent employers, qualifications, responsibilities, or performance metrics. Flag any missing details separately.

Replace the example with your actual experience. Include dates, project details, and outcomes where available.

Treat the instruction against inventing information as a starting precaution. You’ll still need to check the generated text yourself.

Review what the draft says about you

Read the first version before spending time on its appearance.

Can someone unfamiliar with your background understand your contribution? Is the most relevant experience easy to find? Does the summary sound appropriate for your experience level?

Compare the descriptions with your original notes, especially where the wording sounds more confident or impressive.

A rough note might say:

“Helped prepare weekly campaign reports.”

If it accurately reflects your work, a clearer version would be:

“Compiled weekly campaign data in Excel for the marketing team’s performance reviews.”

The revision explains the task and its purpose. Adding “increased campaign revenue by 40%” would require evidence.

Pay attention to verbs, too. “Supported,” “coordinated,” and “led” describe different levels of responsibility. If you assisted someone who owned the project, the wording should make your contribution clear without giving you their role.

You can also remove statements that sound polished but reveal little. A phrase such as “motivated professional with excellent communication skills” takes up space that might be better used for a concrete example.

Choose a layout after the content is settled

NoteGPT’s product page describes template selection as part of preparing your resume for download. Choose a template after you’ve decided which experiences and details you want to keep. See the resume-building workflow.

A design that looks spacious with sample text may feel crowded once you add a long project name or several relevant responsibilities.

Check the document with your own content in place:

  • Are section titles easy to find?
  • Can you comfortably read the text without zooming in?
  • Do the most relevant experiences receive enough space?
  • Do page breaks split information that belongs together?

If a section feels crowded, look for repetition first. Two bullets may describe the same responsibility, or an older role may contain details that don’t help this application.

Reducing the font size should come after those content decisions.

Before investing time in final formatting, check the download options available to you against the employer’s submission requirements. Then open the exported file and inspect it. Confirm that contact details, links, spacing, and page breaks appear correctly.

Make the next revision specific

Once you have a usable draft, avoid treating every revision as a reason to start over.

Choose the problem you want to solve. Perhaps the summary is too broad, a project description doesn’t explain your contribution, or an unrelated job takes up too much space.

Make that change, then read the surrounding section again.

For another application, return to your master notes and the new job description. Decide which experience should move forward and which can be shortened. Keep employers, dates, qualifications, and responsibilities consistent across versions.

If you’re still at the blank-page stage, start with five factual notes about your experience and one target role. Use them to create a draft in NoteGPT, then review the section most relevant to that position.

Before submitting, read each line with one question in mind: “Could I explain this clearly if an interviewer asked about it?”

A finished resume should give you a confident answer.
